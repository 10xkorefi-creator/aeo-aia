Understanding GSTR-2B: The Foundation of Input Tax Credit Claims

GSTR-2B is a read-only, auto-drafted monthly ITC statement on the GST portal. It’s static—once it’s generated for a month, the data stays unchanged—so it becomes the clean benchmark for what ITC is available to claim.

GSTR-2B reconciliation simply means matching your purchase register/books against this statement, then fixing mismatches before you file GSTR-3B (because 2B is what you’re expected to use for the “right ITC” in 3B). Done well, it helps you claim maximum eligible ITC without panic before deadlines—and reduces the risk of errors that can trigger follow-ups later.

Common Challenges in Manual GST Reconciliation

Manual GSTR-2B reconciliation isn’t just “time-consuming”, it breaks down because 2B is a period-wise statement that’s only available after it gets generated, so any late/incorrect supplier reporting quickly turns into mismatches you have to chase.

Why manual reconciliation fails in real life:

  • Too many invoices, too little time: Line-by-line matching doesn’t scale once volumes cross a few hundred invoices per month—especially near filing deadlines.
  • Multi-GSTIN complexity: Separate matching, exception tracking, and follow-ups per GSTIN makes manual work error-prone fast.
  • Vendor reporting issues: Wrong GSTIN, invoice number formats, late uploads, or amendments lead to repeated back-and-forth.
  • Last-minute stress = conservative ITC: Teams either miss credits or intentionally under-claim to avoid risk, which hits cash flow.

The 4 mismatch buckets you end up handling every month:

Use these buckets (even if you’re reconciling in Excel) — they map directly to how you resolve issues:

  1. Matched – good to claim
  2. Only in Books – follow up with vendor / check if upload is pending
  3. Only in 2B – check if you missed booking the invoice / vendor billed wrong entity
  4. Partial / Mismatch – invoice/date/value/GSTIN differences that need correction

Quick checklist before filing:

  • Check “Only in Books” invoices: vendor upload pending? wrong GSTIN? wrong invoice number format?
  • Check “Only in 2B” invoices: not booked in purchase register? booked under different GSTIN/entity?
  • Prioritize high-value mismatches first (top 20 invoices often drive most ITC impact).
  • Keep a simple audit trail: what changed + who approved + vendor communication status.

How AI Accountant differs from Masters India and GSTHero

The GST modules of the two suites take purchase data that is already sitting in your ERP or in Excel. AI Accountant starts one step earlier, at the bill. It reads vendor bills and bank statements, predicts the ledger, and writes the voucher to Tally only after someone on your team approves it. Sinnova Tech Engineering handles 120 purchase bills a month in under 1 hour this way. The same product then reconciles that purchase register with GSTR-2B, so the people who booked the bill also clear the mismatch.

A reconciliation run has four steps: choose the GSTIN and return period, fetch GSTR-2B from the GST portal or upload it, upload the purchase register and map its columns, then go through the results by match status. Tolerance levels you set decide how small a difference in date or amount still counts as a match. Invoices missing from GSTR-2B carry forward and are matched when the vendor files in a later month, with a remark showing the original period. Every action is logged with user, timestamp and before/after state, and multiple GSTINs under one PAN sit under a single login. The help guide walks through the flow, and the export is a workbook that includes Table 4 of GSTR-3B.

One point to weigh: AI Accountant covers reconciliation and the Table 4 workbook, and you verify the figures and file the return yourself. If you want GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-9 filed from the same tool, Masters India and GSTHero do that.

Identifying and Resolving Mismatched GST Invoices

Even with automated tools, understanding mismatch types and resolution strategies is crucial.

  • Invoice Number Variations: Different numbering formats or characters in supplier filings.
  • Date Mismatches: Differences between invoice dates and GSTN upload dates.
  • Amount Discrepancies: Calculation errors or varied tax treatment interpretations.
  • GSTIN-Related Mismatches: Registrations filed under unexpected GSTINs or due to business restructuring.
